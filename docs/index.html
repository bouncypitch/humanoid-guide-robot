Wayfinder

Drop it anywhere in a home. It finds its way, and talks you through it. Wayfinder is a digital twin of AGI House, built from a single phone walkthrough, plus a Booster K1 humanoid that uses it to help guests find their way around this big house. Drop the robot anywhere inside: it works out where it is from its own depth camera, talks with the guest, confirms where they want to go, and walks them there, describing what they pass. Gemma runs on-device, with no cloud.

Picture a guest arriving at a big house they've never been to. Maybe they're older, or have low vision, or just don't know the layout. "Where's the restroom?" shouldn't mean wandering through strangers' rooms.

We built a robot host for that moment. Scan the house once with a phone. After that, a humanoid can be dropped anywhere inside, figure out where it is, and walk any guest to any room, talking them through the way.

How we built it in a day
04

Scan the house once, with a phone done

Our open-source capture apps record colour, depth and camera pose as you walk. The mobile app uses ARKit with LiDAR. The Android app uses ARCore Depth on Pixel; it builds but hasn't been tested on a phone yet. Both write the same session format, and both can stream live to the laptop over Wi-Fi.

05

People are masked out done

The house was full of hackers during the scan. The phone segments every person in every keyframe, shown here in solid purple. Those pixels never enter the map, so the twin keeps walls and furniture, not the crowd. The same masks blur people in any image that leaves the laptop.

06

Fuse a 3D digital twin done

Keyframes are fused into one coloured 3D model of the house, levelled so the floor is flat to within 0.05°. Open the interactive 3D viewer to orbit it yourself.

07

Gemma names the places done

Gemma looks at the keyframes and names what it sees, then a second Gemma pass verifies each candidate. 18 landmarks survived, including the kitchen, restroom, stairs, bar, fireplace, couches and TVs. One false landmark was rejected: a "poker table" that was really a medallion in the floor. Each name is pinned to the map using the phone's depth, and its approach point is restricted to floor reachable from the entrance.

08

Run it on the real Booster K1 in progress

Gemma runs on-device on our laptop, linked to the robot, and nothing calls the cloud. Running it directly on the K1's Jetson is a stretch goal we haven't verified. The robot localizes the same way as in the twin: odometry, corrected by matching its depth against the scanned floor plan. No AprilTags are used. You talk to Gemma, and it confirms the goal before the robot moves; the code enforces that. The robot gives spoken commentary as it walks.

"Take me to the restroom"→ Robot localizes from wherever it stands→ Gemma 4 E2B on the laptop confirms the place→ A* route on the scan→ K1 walks, max 0.5 m/s→ Stereo depth matched to the map→ Gemma confirms arrival
  • Demo: drop the K1 somewhere in the house and ask for the restroom. The rehearsed entrance routes (restroom 19.7 m, kitchen 32.9 m) are the fallback.
  • Safety: every command goes through a wrapper that caps speed at 0.5 m/s and allows at most one motion command per second.

How it works

Two systems, like a person: Gemma does the thinking and talking (System 2), a fast navigator does the walking (System 1). Everything in the loop runs locally.

Phone walkthrough→3D twin + floor plan→Gemma names landmarks→Rehearse routes in MuJoCo
Guest's voice + head camera→Gemma 4 E2B on-device: understand, confirm→Localize from depth→A* route + drift correction→Booster walking policy→Spoken commentary
  • Safety: every motion goes through a wrapper capped at 0.5 m/s and one command per second; the real robot is designed to stop within 0.5 s if the laptop goes quiet (tested against the robot simulator, not yet on the K1).
  • Privacy: people are masked out of the map at capture time; faces in shared images are blurred.

Talk to it works in the twin

One more run, start to finish. The robot is dropped by a couch and doesn't know where it is, so it asks. Gemma, running on the laptop with no cloud, understands the guest, confirms the destination, and won't move until she says yes; the code enforces that. Then it walks her to the restroom, pointing out what's on either side.
